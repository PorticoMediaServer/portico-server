You are one of three reviewers working in parallel on Portico's server performance. Portico's aim: a library of millions of items feels like a small one, on modest hardware. Read `/home/justin/projects/Portico/.worktrees/audit-implementation/_program/PROGRAM-BRIEF.md` section 1 (the owner's decisions; browse and search must always answer, never "being prepared"), then the three capacity reports in `/home/justin/projects/Portico/.worktrees/audit-implementation/_program/reports/` (`scale-check.md`, `scale-check-2.md`, `scale-check-3.md`), which measured and fixed a lot already, and the persistence rules in `server/internal/persistence/README.md`.

The owner's instruction: **do a thorough code review first, focused on performance, then implement fixes for what you find.** A long rebuild of a very large library is expected to an extent, but things are still less efficient than they should be. Address other areas that look excessive too, but do not chase this endlessly: fix root causes with clear wins, and stop.

## How to work
1. **Review first.** Read the code paths in your area end to end and write findings into your report before changing code: what is slow, why (algorithm, query plan, write amplification, per-row round trips, lock or write-gate holding, redundant work, cache misses, triggers firing per row, re-reading what was just written), and the expected gain of the fix. Use `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` and Go's profiler (`pprof` CPU and allocation profiles on the benchmark tool) rather than guessing.
2. **Measure the shape, not the finish line.** The benchmark tool from the capacity lanes is `server/internal/catalog/scalebench` (its usage is in its `main.go` and in `scale-check-3.md`). Run sizes like 10 thousand, 50 thousand and 200 thousand, and for long operations run only long enough to see the curve: is the cost per item constant? Does it rise with size? Is the rise steady (linear, n log n) or accelerating? Once you can answer with decent confidence, stop the run. **Never run a one-million-item operation to completion.** Record per-item rates at several points.
3. **Fix**, most impact first, with the smallest sound change; keep every behaviour, restriction, bound and guarantee (browse and search keep answering during rebuilds; restrictions applied inside SQL; the single write gate; crash safety). Bump derivation versions when derived content changes. Add a tier-level or benchmark-backed test for each fix so it cannot regress.
4. **Re-measure** the same shape after your fixes and put before and after numbers in your report.
5. Checks: `GOFLAGS=-p=4 go build ./...` and `GOOS=windows`/`darwin` builds, `go vet` and the tests of the packages you touch. Do not run the whole suite: a final pass does that after all three lanes merge.
6. **Disk and machine**: the machine's disk filled once. Keep everything you create under `/home/justin/projects/Portico/.worktrees/audit-implementation/_program/scratch/<your-lane>/` below 6 GB at all times, delete databases as soon as their numbers are recorded, delete the scratch folder at the end, and use your own `GOCACHE=/home/justin/projects/Portico/.worktrees/audit-implementation/_program/scratch/<your-lane>/gocache` and delete it at the end. Run one large benchmark at a time. The other two lanes run benchmarks on the same machine, so expect some noise and compare like with like.
7. Commit as you go in your worktree with plain-language subjects (no AI or tool names), never push. No subagents (the cheap helper script in the programme brief rule 12 is allowed for bounded jobs, with oversight). Work autonomously; never ask a question; record decisions in your report.
8. Report sections: findings (each with severity, evidence, expected gain), fixes, before and after measurements with the shape answers, what remains and why you stopped there. Final message under 250 words.
