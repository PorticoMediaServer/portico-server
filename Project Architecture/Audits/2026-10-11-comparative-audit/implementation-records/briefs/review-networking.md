## What to look for, in priority order
1. **Security**: any way an outside caller gains something they should not — spoofed forwarded headers from an untrusted peer being believed; a request classed as "home" that is not; rate limits or sign-in policy keyed on an attacker-controlled address; the first-run claim reachable from a cross-site page, a DNS-rebinding page or a non-browser client in a way the brief did not intend; origin checks that accept too much behind a proxy; route proofs, signatures or pairing weakened by accident; the console reserve abusable to starve others; certificate reload accepting a bad pair.
2. **Wrong behaviour**: IPv6 edge cases (zones, mapped addresses, dual-stack binding on each platform), CIDR parsing, defaults inside a container, settings that do not take effect until restart when they should, races in the listener or reload path.
3. **Claims in the report that the code does not meet**, and scope items quietly not done.
4. **Tests that do not test what they claim.**
Do not report style.

Reproduce the important ones where you can. End with a verdict per scope item of the brief: sound, sound with the listed fixes, or not sound. An honest "I found nothing wrong in X after checking A, B and C" is useful. Final message under 200 words with counts by severity and the report path.
