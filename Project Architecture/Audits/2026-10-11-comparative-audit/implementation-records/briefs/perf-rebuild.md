**Your area**: rebuilding derived data (browse rows, sort keys, visibility and restriction classes, search documents and postings, counters, facets, Home and recommendation features) from empty and after a derivation version bump: the derived worker, its generations and the switch-over, checkpoints and batching, and how it shares the single write gate with everything else. Measured so far: a **200 thousand title empty rebuild took 44 minutes** (about 75 titles a second) and a one-million empty rebuild ran for over five hours without finishing; version-bump rebuilds and write-gate holds of up to about 2 seconds were also measured. That is far slower than the work should need. Find out where the time goes (per-title round trips, statements per title, index maintenance on tables being bulk-filled, triggers firing per row, re-deriving the same thing for each member of a group, visibility classes rebuilt per title, fsync per small batch, the gate being given up and re-taken too often or held too long) and fix it. Building a new generation from empty may use bulk methods that are not safe for incremental updates (for example, filling without secondary indexes and building them once at the end, set-based statements instead of per-title loops), as long as readers keep being served from the previous complete generation and crash recovery stays sound. Also check that steady-state incremental derivation after a single title changes costs a small, constant amount independent of library size.
