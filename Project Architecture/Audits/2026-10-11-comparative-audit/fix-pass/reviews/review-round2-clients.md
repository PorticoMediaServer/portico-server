### 29af71d — Bring a Watch Together follower whose seeks are slow back to the group, and leave a buffering one alone (bugs B-13.2 / related B-13.3)

- Verdict: **NEW PROBLEM**
- What it does: Tracks the follower's pending corrective seek, learns its landing drift as a bounded lead for subsequent seeks, and suppresses seek/rate correction while buffering. Stable eight-second seeks converge in the supplied pending-target model, including its missing-flag variant.
- Findings:
  1. **Blocker — `packages/client-core/src/group-session.ts:380–382,395`: the new lead can request the end of the title before the group reaches it.** There is no duration/playable-end input to `GroupPlayer`, and `target + lead` is not bounded. With a 100-second title, a group initially at 90 seconds and an eight-second first seek, the second request at group position 98 is **106 seconds**. Production `PlaybackService.requestSeek` (`packages/client-core/src/index.ts:1333`) clamps this to **100**, which can finish this viewer's title early if that second seek is faster. An inline clamped-player probe with an immediate second seek ended locally at group position 98. The lane's device note acknowledges this unbounded target; it is a new behavior, not merely an acceptance risk. **Recommend:** expose the playable duration/end and bound latency compensation without seeking to completion ahead of the authority; use a settling/rate strategy near the end. Test variable latency, end-of-title, and group advancement during settling.
  2. **Blocker — `packages/client-core/src/group-session.ts:391–392`: a missing `seeking` flag is inferred from any position movement, which need not mean the seek landed.** An adapter that continues reporting actual playback position while its seek is outstanding satisfies `GroupPlayer`. With eight-second seeks and that position advancing, the real follower issues requests at **0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 seconds**, restarting each pending seek. The new tests hold the pending target constant, so they cannot catch this adapter. **Recommend:** require correlated completion/pending state, or use a fallback that cannot mistake ordinary playback motion for completion. Wire the native adapter's known `pendingSeek` flag; add a no-flag fixture whose old position continues moving until landing, as well as paused and failed seeks.
  3. **Should fix — `packages/client-core/src/group-session.ts:320,393–395`: the 15-second lead cap is not accompanied by a convergence/failure policy.** A deterministic 17-second completion probe with the native-style no-op rate setter repeats seeks at **0, 17, 39, 61, 83, 105 seconds**, retaining two seconds of landing lag after the cap is learned. This exceeds the ordinary video's existing 15-second seek timeout, so it is adapter-contract evidence, not a demonstrated normal video trace. The comment says such a seek “fails”, but no failure is emitted. **Recommend:** define the supported completion bound and report a classified failure when it cannot converge, or use a bounded recovery that converges without a global pause. Test the cap and 20-second settlement timeout explicitly.
- Tests run: Core **1,487 PASS**; native **261 PASS/type-check PASS** on its existing mirrored core. Independent follower probes confirm stable eight-second/two-seek convergence, the out-of-range lead, early completion when clamped with faster landing, the no-flag restart storm, and the capped-latency loop. Buffering suppression is present on the production path.
- Residual risk: Native still omits `seeking` at `apps/native/src/app/together.tsx:33`; its pending-target video model can use the fallback, but the general completion inference remains unsafe. Native's package mirror does not yet contain this follow-up. Physical stream landing behavior and variable network/decoder latency require device traces. The fix appropriately leaves D-45's group-wide wait policy untouched; these local defects need no decision to repair.

- Verdict: **NOT RESOLVED**
