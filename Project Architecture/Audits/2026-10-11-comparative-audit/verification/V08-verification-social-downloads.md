# V08 — Verification of social playback and downloads

## Summary

The first pass largely holds up, with important limits on its runtime and severity claims. All 21 Tier 1 and 17 Tier 2 entries were checked. Tier 1: **3 Confirmed; 15 Confirmed, narrower; 3 Unsettled — needs runtime**. Tier 2: **13 Confirmed; 4 Confirmed, narrower**. No whole entry was refuted; B-14.10's missing-viewer-revision subclaim was refuted by existing database triggers. Twelve Low entries remain explicitly not re-checked.

The strongest confirmations are the Watch Together command-revision race, manual download continuation discarding partial bytes, server retention deleting authorized device copies, unsupported conversion recipes, and receiver controller loss ending Cast playback. Tier 2 B-13.14 was promoted: a local-owner Cast bearer passes ordinary owner administration read authorization, raising it from Medium to High. The report separates genuine control failures from intentional artifact identity, short-lived credentials, inactive-profile privacy and selection-consistency policies. It narrows token-rotation loss to unenrolled container members and drops unsupported frequency/cost estimates. One new Medium finding, N-V08.1, identifies abandoned continuation paging beyond roughly 5,100 members. No Portico application, test suite or infrastructure was run.

## Tier 1 — detailed verification

### B-13.1 Heartbeats and readiness reports invalidate host commands
- **Claim:** Routine presence/readiness writes make an otherwise current host command fail its revision check.
- **What I traced:** The wired routes (`apps/portico-server/server/internal/httpapi/router.go:808`, `social_routes.go:146`) call `internal/social/groups.go:306`. A non-host heartbeat returns without changing presence, but `mutate` still calls `save(..., true)` and advances the revision (`groups.go:597`). Readiness uses the same helper (`transport.go:468`); transport checks that revision (`transport.go:202`). The first-party service heartbeats every ten seconds, adopts only its own heartbeat response, and does not retry transport conflicts (`apps/portico-server/packages/client-core/src/group-session.ts:38,143,222,228`). Readiness events cause re-reads, which reduce but do not remove the race (`:193`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed.** The non-host heartbeat is particularly conclusive: it changes the fence without publishing a group event. The first pass's estimated conflict frequency is unmeasured; no frequency estimate is needed to establish the defect. Existing revision/idempotency tests do not cover this interleaving.
- **Class:** **Defect.** Contract §13 distinguishes group and playback revisions; presence is not a competing command.
- **Severity:** **High.** Hosts of active multi-person rooms can have basic controls rejected during ordinary operation.
- **Fix direction:** Separate semantic command/membership revisions from presence/readiness telemetry, increment playback revision for actual timeline changes, and update fences/parsers together. **Size: M**; `server/internal/social/{groups,transport}.go`, shared `group-session.ts` and protocol tests. Do not blindly retry commands after genuine competing changes.
- **Related:** B-13.5 and B-13.10 share the heartbeat/readiness design; Q-24.18 agrees as an acceptance question. **Backlog:** No specific entry.

### B-13.2 Slow seeks can be repeatedly superseded
- **Claim:** A pending seek is replaced as the moving group clock gets three seconds ahead, preventing a slow seek from settling.
- **What I traced:** Web/native video adapters expose the pending target as local position and phase-ready as readiness (`apps/portico-server/web/src/app/together.tsx:40`; `apps/portico-react-native/apps/native/src/app/together.tsx:32`). `GroupFollower.tick` has no pending-seek guard or correction cooldown (`apps/portico-server/packages/client-core/src/group-session.ts:344`), and it does not suppress correction even if `local.buffering` is true. `seek` reaches `requestSeek`, replaces its revision, and restarts the timeout (`client-core/src/index.ts:1328,1348`); correlated completion requires that new revision (`:1358`). Both providers are mounted in the production shells (`web/src/shell/Shell.tsx:161`; native `src/player/PlayerHost.tsx:62`). I checked the alternative native path: music/books use the native listening owner (`native/src/bridge/useNativePlayback.ts:104`), and `index.ts:1347` bypasses `requestSeek` for that path.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** The source permits this seek storm for the ordinary video path while phase remains ready; it is not proof that every seek over three seconds fails, nor that native audio uses the cited timer. Recovery/starting states, completion latency, native cancellation and group pauses change the result. Existing follower tests use instantaneous mock seeks (`client-core/test/group-session.test.ts:135`).
- **Class:** **Defect.** Contract §13.6 explicitly suppresses corrective feedback while buffering.
- **Severity:** **High.** A slow video seek can repeatedly cancel recovery in a running room; actual affected media/device frequency is unknown.
- **Fix direction:** Expose seek-in-flight/settling state to the follower, suppress correction while seeking/buffering, and correlate one correction through completion before reconsidering drift. **Size: M**; shared `group-session.ts`, both `app/together.tsx` adapters and playback adapters.
- **Related:** B-13.3 is the post-completion version; Q-24.18 agrees. **Backlog:** No specific entry. Cheapest device check: one member plays converted video while a delayed seek takes more than three seconds; log seek IDs and completion. Repeated superseding IDs confirm the practical failure.

### B-13.3 Repeated drift correction after seek completion
- **Claim:** Seek latency leaves a member behind; bounded rate correction then seeks again indefinitely, especially on native where rate nudging is absent.
- **What I traced:** `group-session.ts:350` seeks to the current target without latency compensation; `:351` seeks again if drift remains in the rate band past `correctingUntil`. It checks `at > deadline`, so the nominal four-second window may last until the fifth one-second tick (or a subscription tick). Web multiplies the current base rate (`web/src/bridge/sync-rate.ts:6`); native's group rate setter is empty (`native/src/app/together.tsx:44`). Native video uses this follower, while native listening has a separate seek owner (`client-core/src/index.ts:1347`).
- **Verdict:** **Unsettled — needs runtime.** The nonconvergent conditional is real, but real seek landing positions, buffering pauses and engine completion time decide whether a sustained loop occurs. The claimed 1.15-second web boundary is approximate, not an established threshold; the nudge may last five seconds and base playback speed varies. Cheapest experiment: run one web and one native video member against a playing group, induce stable ~1–2-second seeks, and log target, actual completion position and next correction for thirty seconds. Recurring seeks with unchanged residual drift confirm the loop; convergence refutes that trigger.
- **Class:** **Design risk.** A demonstrated failing device trace would establish the operational defect.
- **Severity:** **Medium** (was High on native / Medium on web). Users on affected slow-seeking streams may suffer recurring interruptions, but universal or ordinary-device failure is not established.
- **Fix direction:** Add a completion/settle window and measure residual drift before choosing another correction; test latency compensation without overshooting. **Size: M**; shared follower, web rate bridge, native video adapter. Preserve the deliberate correction bands.
- **Related:** Merge investigation with B-13.2; Q-24.18 agrees. **Backlog:** No.

### B-13.7 Cast ends playback when the last sender disconnects
- **Claim:** Losing the phone's Cast connection stops otherwise independent TV playback.
- **What I traced:** The production CAF wiring listens for `SENDER_DISCONNECTED` and, only when `getSenders().length === 0`, queues `stopPlayback` (`apps/portico-server/server/receiver/cast/receiver.js:648,655`). That clears playback, stops CAF and deletes the server playback session (`:381`). It does not inspect disconnect reason. The receiver is embedded/published as described in `portico-internal/Project Architecture/04 Playback and Media.md:51`.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Last-sender disconnection stops playback. Losing one sender while another remains does not. Phone sleep is not independently proven to cause that event promptly, and a registered physical Cast deployment has not been exercised.
- **Class:** **Defect.** Contract §7.1.7 and §7.3 distinguish controller loss from Stop.
- **Severity:** **High.** Cast users can lose the film when their last controller goes away.
- **Fix direction:** Keep receiver renewal/progress alive after disconnection; Stop remains explicit, and idle teardown follows playback termination. **Size: S**; receiver disconnect handler and its fixture coverage, followed by physical acceptance.
- **Related:** Q-24.19 agrees; B-13.15 concerns the authority left behind. **Backlog:** Physical pairing/load/reconnect validation is known (`Backlog.md:83`); this stop behavior is not separately listed.

### B-13.15 Unpaired standard LOAD after controller departure
- **Claim:** After the last sender leaves, a new sender can load under the previous viewer's credential.
- **What I traced:** `receiver.js:655` clears `controllerSender`, but `stopPlayback` clears only media state (`:381`), retaining the viewer `session` and `scope`. Standard CAF `LOAD` checks `fromOther` (`:580,584`), which returns false when controller is absent, and `resolvePlayback` needs only that retained session and a valid item ID (`:393`). The custom channel correctly requires the controller (`:614`), so that guard does not protect the standard media channel.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** The source authorization gap exists for a LOAD delivered while the old receiver session remains alive. Availability/duration of that interval on a physical CAF receiver is unverified; it is not an arbitrary account takeover or library-listing capability.
- **Class:** **Defect.** It bypasses the receiver's own pairing/controller rule, rather than a deliberate same-room pause/stop concession.
- **Severity:** **Medium** (first pass Low–Medium). Another sender on the Cast network, knowing an accessible item ID, can start playback and write viewing activity as the previous viewer.
- **Fix direction:** Require an authenticated current controller for standard LOAD/PLAY/SEEK as for the custom channel, without revoking autonomous playback merely because the sender left. **Size: S**; receiver CAF interceptors and fixtures.
- **Related:** B-13.7/B-13.14 and Q-24.19 agree but have distinct fixes. **Backlog:** Generic physical Cast acceptance only. Device check: disconnect the last sender, then send a standard LOAD before CAF teardown; success under the previous viewer confirms practical reachability.

### B-14.1 Server retention deletes device downloads
- **Claim:** Tidying an unused server claim revokes receipts and deletes completed local files, despite regular app use.
- **What I traced:** `apps/portico-server/server/internal/downloads/worker.go:134` expires ready claims using `used_ms`/`ready_ms`, calls `revokeForPreparation` (`receipts.go:550`), then marks expired. Only grant issue/open update usage (`grants.go:104,201`); successful receipt renewal updates receipt dates, not claim usage (`receipts.go:449`). Client renewal removes owned files on `revoked`/retention refusals (`apps/portico-react-native/apps/native/src/bridge/offline-downloads.ts:113,476`). The baseline default is thirty days (`server/internal/persistence/migrations/0001_baseline.sql:4398`); the server test explicitly expects retention revocation (`downloads/downloads_test.go:591`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed.** Daily receipt renewals do not save the download. This is intentional in that server implementation/test, but conflicts with the approved distinction between server/device retention (Saved/Downloads contract §8.7) and P06's expiry-preserves-bytes decision.
- **Class:** **Defect**, not the deliberate thirty-day check-in trade-off. Loss of permission may delete bytes; server cleanup should not manufacture that permission loss.
- **Severity:** **High.** Ordinary download users lose files on their next online check after the server retention interval.
- **Fix direction:** Decouple artifact retention from offline authorization, keep receipt renewal valid after artifact retirement if current permission remains, and reserve revocation for actual authorization changes. **Size: M**; downloads worker, receipts, actions and device outcome handling. Merely refreshing `used_ms` reduces surprise but retains the semantic coupling.
- **Related:** B-14.15/B-14.16; Q-24.22. **Backlog:** No.

### B-14.2 Continue/Retry discard partial transfers
- **Claim:** Manual continuation requests new grant URLs, causing both native platforms to restart the file.
- **What I traced:** `offline-downloads.ts:424` resumes via `fetch`/`startTransfer`, which always calls `service.grant` (`:412`). `apps/portico-server/packages/client-core/src/downloads-service.ts:213` uses a fresh operation ID; `server/internal/downloads/grants.go:92` generates a new token. Apple compares the complete URL before reusing resume data (`native/Native/PorticoDownloads.m:298`); Android deletes `partial.part` if the URL differs (`native/android/app/src/main/java/tv/getportico/portico/native/PorticoDownloadCenter.kt:140`). The Android Range path therefore has no partial on ordinary manual resume.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** First-party Continue/Try again restart. Automatic iOS retries inside the same OS task can reuse its existing URL within validity; not every network interruption necessarily passes through this manual path. Guards against mixing artifacts are good, but URL equality wrongly stands in for artifact equality.
- **Class:** **Defect.** Approved resume semantics bind bytes/digest, not a particular short-lived credential.
- **Severity:** **High.** Large files on unreliable links repeatedly consume full bandwidth and may never finish through manual recovery.
- **Fix direction:** Persist partials by exact artifact identity, obtain new authorization independently, validate range/length/ETag before appending. Apple may need an application-managed range strategy rather than opaque URLSession resume blobs. **Size: M**; offline bridge and both OS download implementations.
- **Related:** B-14.3/B-14.20; Q-24.22 agrees. **Backlog:** No.

### B-14.3 Ten-minute grants fail later OS re-requests
- **Claim:** An OS-delayed start or resumed request uses an expired transfer URL.
- **What I traced:** `downloads/grants.go:150` rejects an open at/after issue expiry and also outside the first-use replay window; the two limits are independent. The transfer route opens once then streams through `http.ServeContent` (`httpapi/downloads_routes.go:228`), without a mid-body expiry timer. Apple creates background URLSession tasks and maps 401/403/404/410 to `grant_expired` (`PorticoDownloads.m:175,304`); manual recovery is B-14.2.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** A *new HTTP request* after the issue deadline fails, even if the pause was short; an already-open uninterrupted body may run longer than ten minutes. The title's “any pause longer than ten minutes” obscures the actual issue-time fence. Specific iOS waiting/retry scheduling and address migration need device evidence.
- **Class:** **Defect** in transfer recovery integration; short-lived grants themselves are deliberate.
- **Severity:** **High.** Deferred/background large transfers may fail when the OS restarts requests and cannot renew authorization itself.
- **Fix direction:** Renew grants for the same artifact before restarting ranges, integrate OS task recovery with that renewal, and support explicit route changes. **Size: M**; grants, offline bridge, Apple downloader. Preserve bounded credentials rather than simply making them permanent.
- **Related:** Merge implementation work with B-14.2 and Q-24.22. **Backlog:** No. Device experiment: enqueue Wi-Fi-only on cellular, wait eleven minutes, reconnect Wi-Fi; capture whether OS first opens the stale URL (401) or foreground renewal replaces it.

### B-14.4 Offered conversion recipes are rejected by the encoder
- **Claim:** 360p, 480p, 1440p and 2160p choices cannot produce their promised conversions.
- **What I traced:** `downloads/options.go:167` offers source-height-compatible ladder rungs; owners with transcoding enabled receive `requiresPreparation` (`:192`). `preparedmedia/types.go:51` defines the four extra recipes. The production file encoder calls `preparedArgs` (`decoder/prepared_file.go:89`), which accepts only 192-kbps audio and the exact 720p/1080p tuples (`decoder/prepared.go:27,33`). Download preparation observes failed optimization (`downloads/worker.go:451`). Non-owner profiles cannot initiate these conversions, and source height/transcoding settings limit offered choices.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed.** There is no alternate encoder builder on this path. Codec/validator widening alone would not fix the earlier recipe rejection.
- **Class:** **Unfinished feature.** New advertised ladder profiles were added without completing encoder/validation/client support.
- **Severity:** **High.** Owners picking common small-video downloads receive deterministic preparation failure; originals/audio/720p/1080p are outside this particular mismatch.
- **Fix direction:** Either withdraw unsupported recipes immediately or implement the full immutable recipe set and matching H.264 level/validator/client dimensions. **Size: M**; decoder prepared builders/tests, preparedmedia types/worker and shared prepared-media parser (S for withdrawing choices alone).
- **Related:** B-03.14 is a distinct 30-fps conversion policy issue; Q-24.22 concerns transfer, not this producer mismatch. **Backlog:** No.

### B-14.5 Original-download copies consume server storage and serialize work
- **Claim:** Each viewer's Original claim copies and then re-reads the complete file in state storage, delaying other downloads and risking disk pressure.
- **What I traced:** Production sets `SourceSnapshotRoot` under the state folder (`apps/portico-server/server/cmd/server/main.go:533`). `downloads/source_snapshot.go:18` explicitly explains immutable snapshots as protection against mutable library roots; `:24` locks snapshot production, copies/hashes to a temporary file (`:38`), persists the completed snapshot and resets the verification offset (`:111`). `worker.go:65` holds `advanceMu` across this work and later hashes windows (`:515`). Claims/snapshot paths are viewer-claim-specific, not globally deduplicated. Admission honors `maxPreparedBytes` if configured (`downloads/service.go:281`), and snapshot production checks the *whole file size* against free space minus the 2-GiB floor before starting, then checks space every 32 MiB (`source_snapshot.go:26,59`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Copy/re-read/serialization are real, but normal disk exhaustion is not established: both an initial whole-size check and periodic floor checks were missed in the alarming impact framing. Concurrent unrelated writers or inability to measure disk remain residual risks. Copy interruption restarts that copy; completed snapshots survive restart (covered by `source_snapshot_test.go:218`).
- **Class:** **Design risk.** The immutable snapshot is a deliberate correctness measure, not negligent redundant work. Serializing all claim advancement is a costly implementation choice.
- **Severity:** **Medium** (was High design risk). Small state volumes and simultaneous original requests can hit an honest storage refusal or long preparation delays; database-volume exhaustion was not reproduced or demonstrated by this path alone.
- **Fix direction:** Preserve immutable byte identity, hash during copy without a redundant verification pass when custody permits, deduplicate only proven identical artifacts, and move bounded copy work outside global advancement so expiry/capture can continue. Expose/configure the existing storage ceiling. **Size: L** for deduplication/worker restructuring, S for clearer limits/status; downloads snapshot/worker/service and mediaartifact custody.
- **Related:** B-14.1/B-14.27(b) prolong storage retention; Q-24.22 agrees on recovery risk. **Backlog:** No specific entry.

### B-14.6 Background completion can lack an offline receipt
- **Claim:** Bytes completed without online JavaScript receipt acquisition cannot be played offline.
- **What I traced:** Native completion publishes `ready` but does not obtain a receipt (`apps/portico-react-native/apps/native/Native/PorticoDownloads.m:204`). JavaScript calls `obtainReceipt` on the ready transition or next connection (`native/src/bridge/offline-downloads.ts:243,262,436`); that method needs the online API. `startTransfer` stores owner metadata but no receipt (`:412`). `playable` requires both a receipt and verified signature (`:511`). Server issuance needs a server-ready claim, not evidence of device transfer completion (`apps/portico-server/server/internal/downloads/receipts.go:289`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Offline blockage is deterministic *if* completion occurred without online receipt acquisition. The universal overnight/locked-phone scenario is not established because iOS may run JavaScript during some background relaunches. An existing foreground completion/next online launch can supply the receipt.
- **Class:** **Defect.** Background transfer completion should leave an independently usable artifact.
- **Severity:** **High.** Users who leave connectivity after unattended downloads may have complete but unusable files on their trip.
- **Fix direction:** Acquire/verify/persist the exact artifact receipt before starting bytes and retain it across native completion; do not let completion erase it. Handle receipts expiring during unusually long transfers. **Size: M**; offline bridge/start metadata, both native downloaders and ownership/receipt fixtures.
- **Related:** B-14.18 affects the same lifecycle but loses bytes instead; Q-24.22 agrees. **Backlog:** No. Device check: complete a queued transfer while JS has no online execution, disable networking before foregrounding, and check receipt/playability.

### B-14.8 Container-following intent is volatile
- **Claim:** Renewing a token or restarting the app loses the whole-show follower, leaving some prepared titles unfetched.
- **What I traced:** `DownloadsService.tracked` is an in-memory map (`apps/portico-server/packages/client-core/src/downloads-service.ts:32`). `refreshRequests` reads only IDs already in that map (`:154`), so it does not rediscover requests. Native provider is keyed by access token and disposes/reconnects on replacement (`apps/portico-react-native/apps/native/src/app/downloads.tsx:19,30`). Enrollment persists individual wanted IDs, but only from captured request pages it has seen (`native/src/bridge/offline-downloads.ts:328`). Shared rotation publishes a replacement session (`apps/portico-react-native/packages/client-core/src/index.ts:535`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Already-enrolled wanted episodes survive because their wants are durable. Remaining *uncaptured or unenrolled* members are lost to this device's automation; a long preparation alone is insufficient to trigger loss if capture/enrollment finished before rotation. Request/claim state remains on the server.
- **Class:** **Defect.** Device-local explicit container intent lacks durable restoration.
- **Severity:** **Medium.** Large captures, early token rotation or an app restart require manual recovery of remaining episodes.
- **Fix direction:** Key service lifetime to stable viewer identity, read tokens dynamically, and persist/recover owner/device-bound request IDs and enrollment cursors. **Size: M**; native provider, shared downloads service and offline stored-state migration.
- **Related:** B-14.9 shares the lifecycle cause; Q-24.22 agrees. **Backlog:** No.

### B-14.9 Token rotation repeats all receipt renewal work
- **Claim:** Access-token renewal recreates the service and renews every held receipt, with repeated whole-manifest writes.
- **What I traced:** The provider dependencies are token/service/API/viewer (`native/src/app/downloads.tsx:19,31`). Each `connect` refreshes signing keys, starts renewal and progress collection (`native/src/bridge/offline-downloads.ts:223`). Renewal batches every owned receipt, without an expiry-near filter (`:464`); each replacement goes through `setMeta` (`:453`). Apple `setMeta` rewrites `_entries` as one manifest (`native/Native/PorticoDownloads.m:112,354`). Generation fences stop obsolete connection results; that protects ownership but does not avoid the next handshake.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Work scales with receipt count and full-manifest persistence, but “hundreds of megabytes every renewal” is an estimate requiring actual catalogue size. Re-sending progress means pending progress, not an unconditional replay of all history. Signature verification memoizes exact signed content, so changed renewal content must be checked.
- **Class:** **Defect** (unnecessary foreground/background work), not a required account change.
- **Severity:** **Medium.** Large device catalogues incur recurring server writes and flash work on routine credential renewal.
- **Fix direction:** Keep service/connection stable across token replacement and renew only on meaningful reconnect or approaching receipt deadline, batching catalogue persistence. **Size: M**; native provider, offline receipt renewal and OS metadata APIs.
- **Related:** B-14.8 shares the fix; Q-24.22 is adjacent. **Backlog:** No.

### B-14.10 Offline personal-state writes differ from online writes
- **Claim:** Offline progress bypasses normal history, thresholds, song/book rules and explicit-action arbitration; the first pass also alleged missing viewer revision invalidation.
- **What I traced:** Wired sync calls the separate `downloads/progress.go:201` writer. It stores positions and activity, touches `personal_items` only with a supplied watched flag, retains `playback_id` on a progress upsert and does not call play-history/count logic.
