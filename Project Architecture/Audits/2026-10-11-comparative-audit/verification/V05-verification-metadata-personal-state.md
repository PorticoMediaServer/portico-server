## Tier 1 — detailed verification

### B-06.1 Titles that contain year-like numbers are misparsed
- **Claim:** The scanner mistakes title numbers for release years; the matcher can erase an all-numeric or year-prefixed title.
- **What I traced:** Production ingestion associates movies through `apps/portico-server/server/internal/catalog/inventory.go:353` and `catalog/catalog.go:494`. `FilenameTitle` takes the first separated 19xx/20xx token (`catalog/catalog.go:354`); TV folders also use it (`catalog/episodic.go:35`). `metadata/screen_match.go:55` truncates at the first 18xx/19xx/20xx token, including position zero. `screenQueries` drops empty queries at :77 and permits NFO evidence and explicit queries at :88/:92. The live `ScreenStep` calls this matching path (`metadata/service.go:132`, `screen_worker.go:356`). The filename tests cover ordinary years but no numeric titles (`screen_match_test.go:10`).
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** The parser defects are real, but affected titles are not inevitably unmatched: an already accepted identity or manual query can bypass the bad filename evidence, and a numeric title can be rescued by NFO/typed identity evidence. When the scanner has also manufactured a contradictory year (e.g. Blade Runner 2049), local-year conflict checks can still force manual selection (`screen_worker.go:516`); an NFO is not a universal automatic escape. A title's year-like number also need not fall within the regex range. A standalone copy experiment is documented under Evidence boundary.
- **Class:** **Defect**; conservative matching does not authorize destroying input evidence.
- **Severity:** **Medium**; affects commonly named numeric titles during filename-only first matching, with manual repair available and no media loss.
- **Fix direction:** Recognize explicit release-year delimiters/rightmost release evidence, preserve title numbers, and share a consistent parser between scanner and matcher. **Size: M**; `catalog/catalog.go`, `catalog/episodic.go`, `metadata/screen_match.go`, parser fixtures.
- **Related:** B-06.2 is the intentional missing-year rule, not the same defect. No separate parser duplicate found in B-index. **Backlog:** no exact entry.

### B-06.2 Yearless name-only matches require selection
- **Claim:** A name without a year cannot automatically match and the owner lacks a screen-title repair queue.
- **What I traced:** `metadata/screen_match.go:182` adds one strong title signal; :194 adds the year signal; :236 requires two and sufficient confidence. `screen_match_test.go:52` explicitly rejects a verified name-only candidate. Typed IDs score as exact at :168, and NFO year/IDs are accepted as input at :92. Unmatched parents stop child matching (`screen_publish.go:962`). - **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** True for a genuinely name-only new match, not every yearless filename: NFO/typed ID/prior identity can settle it. The number of affected shows cannot be inferred from source.

- **Severity:** **Medium**; owners of yearless, ID-free TV folders may need many individual selections, but the conservative rule prevents wrong identities.
- **Fix direction:** Keep the rule; add a paged owner queue/filter for unmatched, needs-selection and unavailable screen titles, and surface available local ID/year evidence. **Size: M**; `catalog/browse_contract.go`, metadata review projection, library tabs/editor in web and native. **Backlog:** no exact queue entry; its older “Fix Match UI” note is broader.
- **Related:** B-06.1 and B-06.12 aggravate the same repair-discovery gap; their causes differ.

### B-06.3 Provider-art occupied-slot guard binds the wrong identifier
- **Claim:** Integrated provider artwork is repeatedly queued and can replace an unlocked local selection.
- **What I traced:** `metadata/artwork_discovery.go:48` resolves each integer entity to a public ID and :89 calls `seedScreenArtwork`. `artwork_integration.go:73` binds that public `t.ID` against integer `entity_id` in both EXISTS branches. Correct resolution occurs in `artwork_state.go:232` and :241. `queueArtworkCandidate` respects selected locks (:258) but upserts a job for the role (:265). - **Verdict:** **Confirmed.** A completed, unlocked local selection followed by another integrated seeding leaves a provider job able to replace it. - **Class:** **Defect**.
- **Severity:** **High**; a routine metadata wake can silently replace the owner's preferred local poster and repeat downloads. Local files themselves are retained.
- **Fix direction:** Resolve the entity once and bind the integer; add a publication-side local/owner precedence check and a fixture for a completed local selection followed by provider reseeding. **Size: S**; `artwork_integration.go`, `artwork_worker.go`, integration fixtures. **Backlog:** no exact entry.
- **Related:** B-06.24 depends on this for still downloads; the claims agree. B-06.11/B-06.20 concern separate throughput costs.

### B-06.4 Local artwork reaches a permanent 128 MiB intake ceiling
- **Claim:** New unique local artwork stops being accepted when the common retained folder reaches 128 MiB.
- **What I traced:** Production constructs `localmetadata.New(state/local-artwork, …)` at `cmd/server/main.go:435`. `localmetadata/audio.go:110` validates/hashes bytes, returns an existing digest at :123, scans all entries at :126, and refuses a new digest over 128 MiB at :136. Sidecar reads use this store, as do embedded covers (:101). Only temporary files are removed in this package (:143). Provider-art lifecycle uses a different cache root. Administration lists local-artwork in the artwork category, but that category is **not cleanable** (`administration/maintenance.go:36`, :197), so its generic cleanup loop does not rescue it.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** The ceiling applies to new unique bytes; an already-stored cover still works, and audio failures can expose `invalid_local_cover`/`invalid_embedded_cover` (:64/:104). Thus “all local art silently stops” is too absolute. Album-count estimates depend on actual cover sizes.
- **Class:** **Defect**; bounded storage is intentional, but an intake ceiling without a supported retention/recovery path defeats local-art libraries.
- **Severity:** **High**; moderately sized music libraries and local-only video collections can exhaust a server-wide artwork budget permanently.
- **Fix direction:** Give retained local originals reference-aware lifecycle/accounting, retain selected originals, reclaim unreferenced digests and make capacity failures visible; consider configurable capacity. **Size: M**; `localmetadata/audio.go`, artwork retention/reference tracking, administration storage projection. **Backlog:** no exact entry.
- **Related:** B-10.2 agrees on the cap; its suggestion that owner maintenance can prune it is contradicted by the current non-cleanable category. Merge the cap findings with that correction.

### B-06.9 Locale changes can republish and mislabel an old document
- **Claim:** Changing library language/region wakes work without invalidating its held document, and old text can acquire the new locale label.
- **What I traced:** Language updates change policy columns (`metadata/library_language.go:31`); administration updates wake the library (`administration_policy.go:94`, :109), as do configuration triggers (`internal/persistence/migrations/0001_baseline.sql:4197`). Accepted identities use `screenRefreshRecord` (`screen_worker.go:363`). Its remembered-document lookup has no locale predicate (`screen_freshness.go:36`); freshness uses provider/type/ID alone (`freshness.go:106`, :122), returning the remembered body when fresh or budget-denied (`screen_freshness.go:100`, :111). Publication stamps `b.Language`/`b.Region` on fields (`screen_publish.go:420`) and `metadata_language` on the entity (:442). Region certification is recomputed from retained country-tagged certifications (:375). The region-change test explicitly deletes freshness before checking publication (`screen_facts_test.go:485`), so it does not disprove this cache case.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Language stale-text relabelling is real when a freshness row exists; a first-match document without one will refetch on its next accepted-identity wake. Region changes can correctly select another certification from an existing multilingual/country-tagged document and do not invariably need refetching. Expiry is permission to fetch on a later wake, not a guarantee of an automatic fetch at that instant; budget fallback can also prolong the issue.
- **Class:** **Defect**; contradicts metadata contract §5.1's actual-returned-locale requirement.
- **Severity:** **Medium**; owners changing language can see stale text presented as the new locale; locked/NFO values have separate precedence.
- **Fix direction:** Key stored documents, validators and freshness by the request locale/region variant; invalidate/requeue the affected variant on policy change, preserving actual returned locale separately. **Size: M**; freshness schema, `screen_freshness.go`, `screen_publish.go`, language/policy mutation and fixtures. **Backlog:** no exact entry.
- **Related:** B-06.10 is the missing force-refresh escape; B-06.24 bypasses freshness for episodes. Those findings agree on which path caches documents.

### B-06.12 Retryable failure can park a title indefinitely
- **Claim:** Six failures, including waiting for provider cooldown, make a work row unavailable with no automatic network-recovery retry.
- **What I traced:** Active claims include only pending/searching/pending_children (`metadata/screen_worker.go:32`). `failScreen` computes 30×2^attempts seconds (:197) and parks at prior attempts ≥5 (:222); Retry-After can enlarge delay. `screenBeforeRequest` returns retryable 429/provider_backoff during a retained cooldown (:259). Its errors reach `failScreen` via acquisition (:365) or episode handling (`screen_publish.go:970`). Owner retry resets attempts (`screen_repair.go:159`). Configuration/source/NFO triggers can requeue rows (`internal/persistence/migrations/0001_baseline.sql:4197`, :4525), but there is no network-online trigger or claim of unavailable rows. These generic wakes do not themselves reset attempts.
- **Verdict:** **Confirmed, narrower.** Six rapid failures fit 30+60+120+240+480 = 930 seconds plus request/worker time, but timeout, Retry-After and queue contention make “a quarter-hour outage” non-universal. “Permanently” means until some authorized wake/retry, not irrecoverable. Existing valid metadata is retained rather than erased.
- **Class:** **Defect**; stopping an infinite retry loop is intentional, treating transient network/cooldown failures as terminal without recovery is the defect.
- **Severity:** **Medium**; first import or a refresh can leave titles stranded after an outage; no media or accepted metadata is destroyed.
- **Fix direction:** Separate terminal publication/validation failure from transport retry, defer cooldown rows without burning attempts, and schedule sparse capped recovery with attempt reset after successful provider recovery. **Size: M**; `screen_worker.go`, work-state schema/policy and retry fixtures. **Backlog:** no exact entry.
- **Related:** B-06.2's absent screen repair queue hides these rows; B-06.13 governs manual recovery priority.

### B-06.13 Fix Match search has no foreground priority

- **Fix direction:** Add bounded owner-search priority ahead of bulk work while retaining fairness and provider cooldown. **Size: S**; claim ordering in `screen_worker.go`, search job priority/fairness fixtures. **Backlog:** no exact entry.
- **Related:** B-06.11/B-06.24 increase the delay; B-18.6 concerns a different refresh action.

### B-06.15 Removed NFO fields keep automatic-provider ownership blocked
- **Claim:** An NFO-sourced field survives removal of its tag or NFO and continues to block provider replacement.
